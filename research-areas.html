Research Areas

Five threads run through this work, all of them asking versions of the same question: how do things spread through a population, and what can we do about it. Each card links to the publications in that area.

Epidemiology

Building models that follow an outbreak through a real population, then using them to answer the questions decision makers actually ask: how many cases are coming, when will hospitals fill up, and which combination of vaccines, closures, and testing will help most. Much of this work ran in real time during COVID-19 and every flu season since, feeding forecasts and scenario projections to public health agencies while the answers still mattered.

Evolution & ecology

Pathogens and pests do not hold still. New variants appear and take over, invasive insects and weeds arrive along trade routes and settle into new places. This work couples the spread of an organism with its evolution, so that models of genomic surveillance can flag a rising variant early, and models of food and commodity flows can show which crops and regions an invasive species is likely to reach next.

Migration & mobility

Where people go shapes what spreads and who needs help. Using anonymized movement data, travel surveys, and census records, this work reconstructs how populations move at scales from a single campus to the whole planet. The same machinery drives two kinds of questions: how commuting and travel carry a virus between places, and how conflict displaces people, as in agent-based models of forced migration from Ukraine.

Social & information networks

Ideas, rumors, and behaviors travel along social ties much the way an infection does, and the structure of those ties decides what catches on. This thread studies influence and content spread in online and mobile networks, competition between messages for limited attention, and how collaboration patterns evolve in science. Those same network models then feed back into public health, for instance in choosing whom to vaccinate first when doses are scarce.

Methods & machine learning

The machinery underneath everything else: agent-based simulations large enough to represent every person in a country, machine learning and ensemble methods that combine many imperfect models into one better forecast, and honest accounting of how uncertain the answer is. It also covers the plumbing that makes such work usable in a crisis, from high performance computing workflows and open benchmarks to recent experiments with AI agents that help set up and interpret epidemic models.
